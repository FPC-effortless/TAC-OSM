## Source-derived, not guessed, native recurrence

Actual `NativeStreamingTAC076._advance` for every observed admitted source byte has
`N_(t+1) = N_t P_t + Q_t`, where
`P_t = α_t(I - β_t w_t w_tᵀ)`,
`α_t = .995*sigmoid(native_gate(e_t)[0])`,
`β_t = sigmoid(native_gate(e_t)[1])`,
`w_t = previous_key` normalized to L2<=1, and `Q_t=β_t*v_t*w_tᵀ` for the same source input. (Original source t0 admission conditions preserved.) Gate P, writer w, and Q do **not** depend on old native matrix. Thus two streams seeing **the exact same real publisher byte and clock** while differing only at clock128 native matrix obey
`Δ_(t+1)=Δ_t P_t`, because Q cancels. With 0<=β<=1 and ||w||₂<=1, `||P_t||₂<=α_t`; therefore in real arithmetic `||Δ_(128+d)||_F/||Δ_128||_F <= product_(t=128)^(127+d) α_t`. Along normalized write-key direction the attenuation factor is `α_t(1-β_t)`; orthogonal directions retain factor α_t. This is **only a bound on the influence of the original native-only initial state**, not retention of all old information, which could live in aligned, CED, wave, upper and cumulative basis recurrences.

Every new original source stream recalculated exact source P at every clock and required `Δnext-Δprev P` max absolute <=1e-4, plus observed difference norm <=product alpha +1e-4 (explicit FP32 cancellation tolerance); all nine passed. The Python/Lean companion formally verifies scoped scalar same-source difference and scalar directional retention, and independently tests small full matrix double Frobenius behavior. The full k×k operator norm theorem in Lean, whole-source FP32 IEEE, AD/backprop, mask/parallel scan and full model computation-graph mathematical correspondence remain OPEN.

## Interpretation: source retention mechanism is concrete, not just a vague interference hypothesis

The native source has a **multiplicative gate α≈0.5–0.6**, which mathematically contracts any initial native-state difference roughly every byte, and a positive direction-specific β write/erase mechanism. This is a direct **architectural cause of short-lived influence of the specific initial native matrix**, even if key collisions were eliminated. The larger native matrices m12/16 have slightly **shorter α half-life bounds** than original m8 under the fitted weights, which helps explain why merely doubling matrix size in LANGSCALE075/076 did not reliably improve long-range source accuracy.

Crucial limitation: *this does not prove that information is forgotten by the entire TAC model*. More recent native writes create new native information at every time. Aligned matrices, upper48, CED concepts, waves and cumulative basis can retain source history. It does not establish whether old content is reconstructible from native matrix under a correct learned query, whether longer-context training would learn α closer to1, or whether a new selective slow lane is useful. Angles between normalized query/write keys are only geometry; source event-content retrieval or stable address labeling remains unverified. No new learned model or qualified scaling law/SSM/Transformer comparison, no mobile speed or investor-capability claim.
