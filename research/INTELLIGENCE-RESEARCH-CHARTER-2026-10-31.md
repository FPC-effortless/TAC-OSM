# Intelligence research: theory, mechanisms and implementations

Status: research framework and prospective claims, not an established theory.
TAC-OSM is one experimental implementation; alternative systems can support or
challenge the same claims. Preserve every historical lane and negative result.

## Objects of study

1. Functional theory: what competences a system exhibits under specified goals,
   environment distributions, observations, prior experience and resource limits.
2. Mechanistic theory: which computations and learning processes causally produce
   those competences, and under which conditions alternatives work equally well.
3. Implementation: source-pinned TAC/OSM candidates and standard comparator models.

A definition does not establish a law; an architecture failure does not by itself
refute the broad framework. Each theory claim must state an implementation
correspondence and an outcome that would count against that particular claim.
Do not protect every hypothesis by attributing all negative results to engineering.

## Separate measurements

Report existing competence, experience-dependent acquisition, retention under
delay/interference, transfer across specified novelty, and full resource costs.
Frozen competence is legitimate; online parameter changes are not required on
every task. Learning a newly sampled task rule is distinct from acquiring a new
solution algorithm. Neither is inferred from a successful pipeline or lower loss.

Evidence stages are solvable output execution, acquisition from experience,
causal retention, transfer and mechanistic explanation. They are diagnostic
dependencies, not a ban on parallel development. An unmet earlier stage limits
interpretation at later stages and is reported explicitly.

## Candidate principles and falsifiers

Memory, prediction, explicit reusable representations and adaptive computation
are compatible hypotheses. Mere participation in a successful system does not
establish necessity, sufficiency or optimality. Register which claim is tested.
For each: define the intervention, alternative explanation, resource regime,
quantitative effect threshold and scope before confirmatory outcomes are read.

Define reusable structure narrowly for a given study, such as separately encoded
operations and arguments that recombine on unseen cases. Do not redefine every
successful internal pattern as structure after outcomes are known. Successful
memory on a task deliberately requiring hidden historical information establishes
an information requirement, not a general theory of intelligence.
